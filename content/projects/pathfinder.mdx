## The Problem

Change management across 14 divisions had no shared system. Business units ran the work on their own processes and trackers, so the same question answered differently depending on who was asked, and leadership had no comparable view across divisions for planning.

## The Product Decision

I built Pathfinder, a change-management command center on Dataverse and Power Platform, and got the organization onto it as the one system of record. The product decision had two parts:

1. **One system, three operating processes.** Rather than force 10 business units onto a single process or let each keep its own, I aligned them on three operating processes the command center supported natively. Enough standardization to make reporting comparable, enough flexibility that units adopted it.
2. **Reporting designed to run on one analyst.** The reporting model was built so a single analyst could operate it rather than a reporting team, and its output fed C-suite planning directly.

## What I Owned

I built Pathfinder and ran it from launch in 2020 through 2023 as its product owner while at Microsoft. I managed the seven-person vendor team that delivered it, built the governance model, aligned the 10 business units on the three operating processes, and designed the reporting.

## What Shipped

A command center used by roughly 1,000 change managers, program managers, and sponsors across 14 divisions. Change work lived in one governed system: the operating processes ran inside it, and the reporting it produced was the input to executive planning.

## Results

1. About 1,000 users across 14 divisions ran their change work through the product.
2. 10 business units aligned on three operating processes, replacing per-unit tracking.
3. The reporting model ran on one analyst instead of a reporting team, and its output fed C-suite planning.

## Microsoft Ecosystem Fit

Pathfinder ran on Dataverse and Power Platform inside the organization's existing identity, security, and governance. Users worked in the tools they already had, which is a large part of why adoption held across 14 divisions.

## Completion

I ran the product through 2023 and handed it off when I left Microsoft in January 2024. Pathfinder predates my AI-native build approach; it is the enterprise product ownership that approach now sits on top of.
