## The Problem

A central team manages swag for seller events at a company with more than 5,000 account managers and sellers. The team handles about 90 events a quarter, 7-8 a week. Each event is hosted by an account team for customers ranging from C-suite executives to technical staff, and each attendee tier gets a different level of swag. Every event draws from one budget that has to last the full period, and the budget, inventory, allocation rules, and orders were tracked in separate spreadsheets.

## What I Owned

I worked with the event operations team to define the problem, proposed a product with one interface for requesters and one for the operations team, designed the budget and allocation workflow, and directed the build. The product is in final testing with the operations group, and I own governance, support, compliance, and product changes through launch and after it.

## The Product Decision

The product had to be built on Power Platform, with Copilot Studio for the agent and Dataverse for the data. It also serves two groups with different needs: people requesting swag for one event, and an operations team managing budget and inventory across all of them. I built one interface for each, on one shared Dataverse backend:

* A **Copilot Studio agent** for requesters, who describe the event in plain language.
* An **admin Code App** for the operations team, with dense screens for budget, inventory, and orders.

## What I Built

**The agent.** Someone describes an event in natural language; the agent proposes a swag package, negotiates within budget, and finalizes it. The budget optimization is portfolio-aware, allocating against the program's remaining budget across all events rather than just the one in front of it. The negotiation math lives in Power Fx, with two Power Automate flows handling context loading and finalization.

**The admin app.** A Vite, React, and TypeScript app in Fluent UI, modeled on Azure Cost Management: an events-first budget dashboard, a packages editor, personas managed as data, inventory, and orders. Budget health shows green, yellow, or red based on remaining budget as a share of the program total.

## Budget Negotiation and Editable Tiers

Two problems took the most work, and both came partly from building on Power Platform.

1. **Budget negotiation inside Copilot Studio.** In Copilot Studio, an agent's logic has to be built from topics, flows, and Power Fx. To have the agent propose a package, stay within budget, and negotiate down, I put the optimization math in Power Fx and kept the agent's record of the remaining budget correct through the whole conversation.
2. **Editable attendee tiers.** The attendee tiers (C-suite down to technical staff) started as a hardcoded TypeScript type, so every rename or regrouping of a tier needed a code change and a deploy. I moved the tiers into data, so renaming a tier in settings updates it everywhere without a deploy. The operations team can now change tiers themselves instead of filing a ticket.

## Building on the Microsoft Stack

Storage is Dataverse, the people picker uses Entra, access uses Dataverse security roles, and Power Automate runs the flows. Because of that, the product uses the organization's existing sign-in, permissions, and governance.

## Results and Status

The product covers about 90 events per quarter and is estimated to save close to $250K a year in specialist time, based on the allocation and negotiation work it removes from the central team. It is in final testing with the operations group before launch.

## The Tradeoff

Building on Power Platform cost me flexibility: the agent's budget logic had to fit inside Copilot Studio topics and Power Fx. In exchange the product got the organization's identity, permissions, and governance without my building them. For a tool that has to run inside an existing organization, I would make the same choice again.
