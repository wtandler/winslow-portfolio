## The Problem

A rates portfolio manager (PM) forms a view on Fed policy and rates each morning from sell-side research from a dozen banks, economist Substacks, Fed releases, NBER and SSRN papers, prediction-market odds, and the data releases. All of it is public but spread across dozens of sources. Large funds pay research teams to read it, and the reading still takes hours every morning.

## The Product Decision

I grouped the sources into four views and built the product around where they disagree:

* What **markets** say: Polymarket and Kalshi odds on Fed / CPI / NFP.
* What **dealers** say: the NY Fed's Survey of Market Expectations.
* What the **Street** is writing: bank desks, economists, independent researchers.
* What the **data** says: my own model.

The product shows where the four disagree, using only public data, so a PM can see the disagreements without doing the morning reading.

## What It Does

* **Today's View**: my model's forecast and a one-paragraph explanation, written in the voice of a senior strategist.
* **Consensus vs Our View**: Markets / Dealers / Street / Our Model, side by side, with divergence flags.
* **Event Feed**: a real-time feed of Fed events, data releases, rate moves, and new research in time order, each summarized by AI with a link to its source.
* **Prediction Markets**: Kalshi and Polymarket probability bars on the relevant contracts.
* **Street Synthesis**: firm-by-firm positioning (Barclays / Goldman / JPM / …) with consensus-vs-divergence flags.
* **Morning and Close Notes**: twice-daily synthesis (7:00 AM ET pre-open, 4:15 PM ET post-close), emailed to subscribers.

## Sourcing and Checking

Most of the work went into collecting and summarizing public sources reliably. Pulling structured signal from DTCC, OFR, FINRA TRACE, NY Fed percentiles, prediction-market APIs, and free-form sell-side commentary means a custom adapter per source and a clustering step to dedupe the same idea showing up in ten places.

Every summarized claim links to its source, because a PM will not trust a number they cannot trace. The model work runs in tiers with a cross-model check: Claude Haiku for fast, event-driven Tier 1 synthesis, Claude Opus for the scheduled morning/close notes and position extraction, and a GPT pass as cross-model confirmation on Tier 1 outputs before anything publishes.

## Current Work

I am extending the review process beyond fact checking with a senior trader critique that challenges each note's market view, trade logic, risks, supporting evidence, and what would change the conclusion.

## The Architecture

A Python worker collects sources, groups duplicates, writes the summaries and notes, and saves them to Postgres (Neon). The Next.js site on Vercel only reads from Postgres, which keeps pages fast. GitHub Actions runs every scheduled job, so there are no servers to keep running. Email goes out through Resend. There's an admin research-intake console behind HTTP Basic Auth for pasting, uploading, and overriding failed notes.

## Building with AI

I built the first version in a weekend with Claude Code, using the workflow I developed on Threadbase. AI summarization lets one person cover reading that a research desk would normally split across several people.
